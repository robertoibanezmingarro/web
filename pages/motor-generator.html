Motor–Generator System Identification and PI Control

Control Systems · System Identification · Embedded Experimentation

Full experimental identification of a real motor–generator chain, from black-box data collection to control-oriented modeling. The workflow compares excitation signals, model orders, and sampling times to retain a practical first-order plant. Design choices are driven by robustness, hardware limits, and implementation-ready control decisions.

  • System identification
  • MATLAB/Simulink
  • National Instruments
  • Frequency analysis
  • Discrete PI control
Role
Control and experimentation engineer
Scope
Identification campaign, model validation, and closed-loop control
Focus
Model simplicity vs control usability under real constraints
Output
Validated PI regulation around a hardware-limited operating point

On this page

    Global Architecture / Experimental Setup

    The setup closes the loop between MATLAB/Simulink and National Instruments hardware for repeatable excitation and synchronized acquisition. This architecture enables direct transition from test-bench measurements to control-oriented data processing.

    Identification Methodology

    The campaign evaluates MLBS, chirp, and multisine excitations over sampling times of 0.05 s, 0.01 s, and 0.002 s. Model orders 1, 2, and 3 are estimated under the same protocol to isolate excitation and discretization effects.

    Model Validation and Signal Comparison

    Time-domain overlays show stable fitting with chirp and the strongest consistency with MLBS, while multisine remains less reliable. Despite higher-order candidates, the first-order model is retained as the best engineering trade-off between fidelity and deployability.

    Frequency-Domain Analysis

    Bode inspection confirms first-order dominant behavior and supports reduced-order control design. Cross-checks across excitation families prevent overfitting to one test profile and improve confidence in selected dynamics.

    Load Influence Analysis

    Load changes modify both static gain and pole location, proving that the plant cannot be treated as load-invariant. This observation drives conservative controller tuning and operating-point-aware validation.

    Control Strategy (PI)

    Since the plant has no integrator, PI control is selected to guarantee zero steady-state error on output voltage regulation. Tuning prioritizes robustness and actuator realism over aggressive transient targets.

    Experimental Validation

    Closed-loop tests confirm disturbance rejection under variable loading, with regulation centered around 9 V due to hardware limits. The 12 V target was intentionally relaxed to preserve repeatability and avoid non-representative stress conditions.

    Closed-Loop Response

    Hardware-in-the-loop execution confirms stable voltage tracking and practical robustness to step-like load perturbations. Results align with the first-order design assumptions without requiring model complexity escalation.

    Engineering Takeaways

    • Excitation signal design strongly conditions identification quality and downstream control reliability.
    • First-order modeling can outperform higher-order alternatives when implementation speed and robustness matter.
    • Sampling-time sensitivity must be quantified early to avoid misleading parameter interpretations.
    • Real hardware constraints, including voltage limits, should shape the final regulation strategy.
